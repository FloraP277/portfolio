Systems Design

Smart-DMV Digital Verification System

My graduate capstone, built with a five-person project team for the Integrated Information Systems Capstone (ISTM 6210) at The George Washington University: a system design that modernizes the DC DMV's pre-appointment workflow.

Tools

Systems Analysis React Tailwind CSS AWS Textract

The problem

The DC DMV serves more than 650,000 licensed drivers through roughly 3,200 daily interactions, and identity verification happens manually at the counter. The result is 45 to 60 minute average waits, a 35% rejection rate with one in three residents turned away over document errors, and 28% of staff time spent on manual checks. The root cause our analysis surfaced: verification happens during the visit, rather than before it.

What the work covers

Our design shifts verification upstream. Residents upload identity documents from the web or their phone, an AI-powered gatekeeper runs automated OCR and confidence scoring on them, and appointments can only be booked after the documents pass, so every resident who reaches the counter is already service-ready. Low-confidence documents route to DMV staff for manual review instead of failing silently.

The Level 0 operational workflow, from login to booked appointment

We modeled the system from the context view down through the data flow diagrams: image quality checks, OCR text extraction, confidence scoring, and PII masking with secure storage. The proposed stack is a React and Tailwind CSS front end for residents with AWS Textract handling text extraction, designed for compliance with REAL ID and District privacy standards.

The outcome

The projected impact: average waits drop from 45–60 minutes to under 15, rejections fall from 35% to under 15%, manual verification shrinks to a 20–30% exception workload, and daily throughput gains 35% capacity. Against an estimated $80k–120k build cost, the annual savings of $150k–200k mean the system pays for itself within its first year of operation.
