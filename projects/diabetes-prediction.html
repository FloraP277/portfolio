Diabetes Prediction with a Neural Network

Can diagnostic measurements alone tell you whether a patient has diabetes? I trained a neural network on the Pima Indians Diabetes dataset to find out.

Tools

Python Keras pandas scikit-learn seaborn

The problem

The dataset comes from the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases. Every record describes a patient through measurements such as glucose level, blood pressure, BMI, and age, together with a label saying whether the patient was diagnosed with diabetes. The goal is a model that predicts that diagnosis for a new patient.

My approach

I started with exploratory analysis to understand the shape and statistics of every column before touching a model. Because neural networks are sensitive to the scale of their inputs, I standardized the features with StandardScaler so that glucose readings in the hundreds would not dominate features measured in single digits.

The model itself is a Keras Sequential network of dense layers with Dropout between them to keep it from memorizing the training set, trained with the Adam optimizer. I plotted the loss live during training, which makes it obvious the moment the model starts overfitting rather than discovering it after the fact.

Compiling and training with live loss plotting

The outcome

The network learned to separate diabetic from non-diabetic patients from the diagnostic measurements alone. Just as valuable was what the process taught me about regularization in practice. Watching the training and validation curves diverge, then adding dropout and watching them come back together, made the concept concrete in a way no textbook explanation had.
